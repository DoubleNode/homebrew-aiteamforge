## What Space Dock Is and Is Not
Space Dock exists for **this machine, right now** — LCARS won't stay up, an auto-upgrade stalled,
items are orphaned after an unclean shutdown, disk or log pressure, a stranded worktree. It fronts
tooling that already exists on this box (`aiteamforge-doctor.sh`, `kb-recover`,
`lcars-health-check.sh`, `kb-sweep-stubs`, the auto-upgrade logs) — it does not reimplement
diagnostics, and it is not a second team's feature work in disguise. If what's in front of you is a
product defect rather than a machine fault, that's Academy's ticket, not Space Dock's. Escalate
it by filing a real ticket on the Academy board through the Project Planner skill — mandatory for
all ticket creation, no exceptions. The XSDK item stays where it is as the incident record and
references the new ticket id; it is never promoted or migrated. The boundary between Space Dock
and Academy is the board boundary.

## Space Dock's Board Is Per-Machine
The kanban board at `~/.aiteamforge/spacedock/kanban` is local to this box — it is not
git-tracked and has no repo. `XSDK-*` item ids **collide by
design** across machines: `XSDK-0001` on this box and `XSDK-0001` on another mean two different
incidents. Never treat an id as fleet-unique, never try to reconcile boards across machines, and
never assume the healthy-fleet assumptions from Academy's kanban apply here — this board has no
remote, and nothing about it should be authored as if it does.

When a reference LEAVES this machine — a `kb-msg`, an escalation note, a PR body, a chat message —
qualify it with the host: `<hostname>/XSDK-nnnn`. Lowercase short hostname first, single slash, id
second: `m4mini/XSDK-0001`. A bare `XSDK-nnnn` is perfectly fine on-host; the prefix becomes
mandatory the moment the reference could be read somewhere else. `m4mini/XSDK-0001` (an LCARS
crash-loop) and `m1pro/XSDK-0001` (a stalled auto-upgrade) are two unrelated incidents that happen
to share a bare id. Qualify it once it leaves the box.
